How Long Should You Rest Between Sets? Guidelines Without Being Ruled by a Timer

October 29, 2026

Have you ever wondered how long to rest between sets? Some people assume shorter rest always works better, but resting too little can drop the weight or reps on your next set and actually lower the quality of your training.

How rest affects your numbers

With short rest, you start the next set before your muscles and breathing have recovered. As a result, your reps at the same weight tend to drop, and the total work you get through — your volume — shrinks. Recent research has also reported better hypertrophy results with moderately longer rest compared to very short rest.

Guidelines by goal

  • Heavy compound lifts (squat, bench press, deadlift) at low reps: 2–5 minutes. Your nervous system needs time to recover too
  • Moderate weights for hypertrophy (around 8–12 reps): 1.5–3 minutes
  • Isolation exercises (curls, lateral raises, and so on): 1–2 minutes. Smaller muscles tend to recover faster

The rest you need changes with how you feel that day and where an exercise falls in your workout, so treat these numbers as guidelines.

Go by recovery, not a fixed number of seconds

Setting a timer for "next set at 90 seconds" keeps your pace steady, but it can also rush you when you haven't caught your breath yet. Especially on heavy sets, starting before you've recovered can break down your form and lead to injury.

A good rule is to start when your breathing has settled and you feel you can hit your target reps. On top of that, knowing how long you actually rested helps you catch the opposite problem too — like realizing you've been scrolling your phone for over five minutes.

Tips for keeping track of rest

  • Make the time since your last set visible at a glance
  • If a set drops off sharply from the previous one, check whether your rest was too short
  • If your workouts are running long, try trimming rest on isolation exercises first
